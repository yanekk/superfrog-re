# forest-dumb-translator — delivery report

## What was delivered

**The whole of Forest Dumb Forever now runs as translated Rust, from power-on, in the app, with sound.**

- **The translator works on the whole program.** All 7 716 instructions of the game are turned into Rust automatically, with no hand edits and no hints. Every one of the 291 kinds of instruction the game uses was checked against a reference processor on over 1.6 million test states, including timing, and all match. All 100 routines that can be checked on their own match the original.
- **The game runs on a pretend Amiga built from the project's own chip models**: memory, screen, drawing hardware, sound, joystick, interrupts and the operating-system calls the game makes. The pretend Amiga now also models the screen hardware slowing the processor down, which turned out to matter (you approved this in T46/T49).
- **Level 1 matches the original on 49 of its 50 test runs**, both translated on its own and with level 1's hand-written engine plugged in. The one failure is the run from power-on all the way into level 1 (below).
- **Worlds 2 to 6 each start and play their first stretch exactly as the original**, 5 of 5.
- **The app** starts at the disk prompt, goes through the title and menus into the levels, plays the title music and level 1's sound effects, and runs level 1 on the hand-written engine. If the translation ever meets something it can't run, the picture freezes, says where, and saves a bug recording. Choosing KONIEC on the title quits the app. `--engine hand` still opens the old hand-written level 1 for comparison.
- **The translator is now the first thing a session reaches for.** Three fresh sessions given a porting job each used it without being told. Haiku (the cheap Claude model) writes test generators and first drafts of rewrites, as you chose; Muse and its OpenRouter tools are gone.

**Not delivered:**

- The power-on-to-level-1 test run still differs from frame 6 301, on the translated program alone; nobody found the cause.
- 11 of the 100 routines differ when checked on the pretend Amiga's chips. All 11 are explained by the test harness's simplified hardware, not the translation, and are named.
