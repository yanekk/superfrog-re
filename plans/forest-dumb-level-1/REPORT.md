# forest-dumb-level-1 — delivery report

## What was delivered

**Level 1 of Forest Dumb Forever is playable from start to end in its own app.** All 86 tasks are done (the 50 planned, plus 36 added during the build). The tests are green and the branch is in sync with main.

What you get:

- **The app.** It opens straight into level 1, silent, with your keys (Up on Space, Fire on Left Option) and game pad support. The menu has Start Level, Controls and Quit, and Esc quits. Running out of lives shows the original's game over screen, then the level restarts. Finishing the level plays the original's end sequence, shows "Level 1 complete" until you press fire, then restarts.
- **The level itself.** Movement, scrolling, drawing, the status bar, coins and items, lives and the countdown, creatures, springs, water, spikes and vanishing blocks are all rebuilt from the original's code.
- **Proof it matches the original, judged by machine.** One route of 22,464 game steps finishes the level with no life lost on both the rebuilt game running alone and the original, and the two agree at every step. The same route is played in the real app from launch to "Level 1 complete", equal to the headless run on every frame. Many shorter runs cover each behaviour separately.
- **The recorder.** Start Level offers "Enable recorder?". Stop saves one zip, which replays headless and can be compared with the original.
- **The route planner and the map page.** The planner finds the route, and the map page shows the level with the character's path. The map page is built locally and never committed, because it holds the game's graphics.
- **Shared pieces moved into the toolkit.** The app windows, the planner and the map page are now shared by both games. Superfrog's planned routes are pinned and unchanged.
- **Translation by the cheaper model.** Muse translated the original's routines for cents, each one checked against the original's code, and from 6 November on your say it also wrote the engine code for the remaining porting tasks.

What is not delivered, by design: levels 2 to 6, sound, the title screen and menus, high scores, and a pause key.

What matches only within stated limits:

- **Timing finer than one frame is not reproduced** (the plan puts it outside the bar). In a handful of places the original's behaviour depends on exactly where an interrupt lands: the status bar digits updating up to half a second earlier or later, a creature appearing one step later, a creature blinking out for a moment when five are drawn at once. In tests, the comparison measures where the original was cut and accepts only what the original itself could show there. In the playable game these moments can differ slightly from the original.
- **Seven rebuilt routines never run in a whole-level comparison.** Six only run while the level is being set up, before the point every comparison starts from; their result is checked by tests that a fresh game equals the original at that point. One handles a time bonus level 1 does not have. All seven are checked individually against the original's code.
- **Comparing a bug recording made in the app with the original** is reliable only up to the first moment the app ran behind (for the whole route, step 1,340 of 22,464). The headless cases cover the rest.

**Risks**

- **The timing acceptance rules were my decisions, not yours.** They cover the status bar, the scenery redraw, the creature start, the creature blink and one skipped status refresh. A fault in how it kept two machines apart was patched. Both changes rebuild the saved starting points once. Superfrog's checks and pinned routes stayed unchanged.
- **Known differences left open, found by searching unusual inputs.** They are outside the route and the cases:
  - holding Up and Down together;
  - one animation value at one step of a searched script;
  - a creature's slot order when a step is cut inside a creature start;
  - a 133-pixel picture difference at one frame of one test route, which is compared on state only.

  Each is written down with a script that reproduces it.
- **One case misses its goal when the engine runs alone.** The vanishing-blocks case agrees with the original step by step, but the engine on its own does not reach that case's goal on the new script.
- **Some picture coverage is thinner than state coverage.** One creature kind is compared on state only, never drawn. The animated cells' phase is not compared. A column-box exclusion has no test proving the rest of its rows is still compared.
